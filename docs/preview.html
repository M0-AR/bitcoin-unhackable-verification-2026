Stage 1 — Fingerprint: the hash

Idea in one sentence: a hash is a fingerprint for data — change one letter and the whole fingerprint changes. Bitcoin uses SHA-256 (64 hex characters). Type anything and compute its real SHA-256 right here:

Try: change 10 to 90 and press the button. Expect ~half the characters to flip (we measured 62/64 on this pair — avalanche).

Three properties to remember: (1) same input → same output, always; (2) tiny change → unrecognizable output; (3) one-way — you cannot reconstruct the input from the fingerprint. That is why a ledger row can be sealed.

Stage 2 — Chain: why "blockchain"

Every block stores two fingerprints: its own, and the previous block's. Change page 1 and page 2 still quotes the old fingerprint — mismatch, exposed. That is the whole chain.

block 0 (genesis)  prev=0000…0000  hash=3a96…      "network start"
block 1            prev=3a96…      hash=0007…      "Ahmed sent Mohamed 10"
block 2            prev=0007…      hash=0009…      "Mohamed sent Sarah 7"   ← breaks here if block 1 edited
block 3            prev=0009…      hash=000b…      "Sarah sent Yousef 3"

Our validator asks three questions per block — Q1 stored == recomputed? Q2 prev == previous hash? Q3 enough leading zeros? Any "no" = forgery. Verification is O(n), ~0.8 µs/block — cheap to check, expensive to write. That asymmetry is the trick.

Stage 3 — Mining: why the electricity

Computing a hash is trivial — so rewriting would take seconds. Bitcoin's fix: a block counts only if its hash starts with enough zeros. No shortcut exists; the machine brute-forces a meaningless number called the nonce, like a suitcase lock. Try it (toy difficulty):

Press mine — your laptop does what billion-dollar farms do, just slower.

Measured law (this repo, 10 trials): difficulty 1→28 hashes, 2→388, 3→4,619, 4→66,474 vs theory 16/256/4096/65536 — each extra zero ≈ 16× work (hex alphabet). Local speed ≈ 1.25 MH/s. Scale that to mainnet (2⁷⁹ ÷ 10²¹ H/s) and you get 604.5 s ≈ 10 minutes — the toy predicts Bitcoin's heartbeat. Difficulty retargets every 2,016 blocks (~2 weeks) to hold it.

Stage 4 — Zico attacks (you are the hacker)

Four attempts, exactly as narrated. Click each to reveal the outcome our code measures:

❌ Caught in ~10 µs at Q1 — stored hash ≠ recomputed. Instant.

❌ Caught at Q3 — fresh hash has no leading zeros, plus Q2 breaks downstream.

⚠️ Block 1 passes, block 2 fails Q2 — you must re-mine every later block. The chain drags you forward.

✅ Passes on the toy in milliseconds–seconds — honest admission! Small chain + small difficulty = hackable. Security comes only from Stage 5: scale + copies.

Stage 5 — The secret: thousands of copies

There is no single ledger. The grocer, Umm Ahmed, the pharmacy — thousands of nodes each hold a copy, and all follow the longest valid chain (most accumulated work). While Zico re-mines history, honest nodes extend the tip (7 blocks vs his 5) — his valid forgery is rejected for being shorter. To win he must out-hash the planet continuously (>50%), i.e. billions in ASICs + national electricity — and success would crash the price of the loot. Attack cost ≫ attack value. That is the genius: security from economics, not passwords.
